Teaching

My goal in a course is less about what you learn than about how you learn to think: reading a dense text closely, noticing what it leaves unsaid, and writing mathematics that someone else can check. I want graduates who can take on research by themselves and who communicate mathematics well.

What I believe about it

In Arabic, my first language, a student is ṭālib al-ʿilm (طالب العلم): a seeker of knowledge. Seeking takes will, and where there is will there is a way. I agree with László Polgár that geniuses are made, not born.

  • Less can be more. A few foundational topics with plenty of practice teach more than a survey of many, especially in introductory courses.
  • It is fine to go slowly. Gaps in the assumed background make a subject look harder than it is, and closing them is usually not much work.
  • Writing is part of the subject. Communicating mathematics, in writing above all, matters as much as the technical content. The first step in solving a problem is stating it clearly.
Teaching to solve problems is education of the will.George Pólya, How to Solve It

How it shows up in my courses

  • Writing is assessed, not assumed.In CSC236, one question on every assignment is graded on a three-part writing rubric, so students get feedback on how they argue as well as on whether they are right. In CSC373, a writing task is worth 10% of the grade.
  • Students help write the assignments.The third CSC363 assignment was built from questions contributed by students and teaching assistants, each credited by name on the assignment.
  • Turing machines are programs you can run.In CSC363, students write and debug Turing machines in Varphi, a language and debugger built by my student Hassan El-Sheikha under my supervision.
  • Guess first, then check.My interactive lessons ask for your answer before they show you anything, and the feedback names the mistake a wrong answer usually comes from.
