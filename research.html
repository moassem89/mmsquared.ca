Research

I am a logician by training. My own work is in computable structure theory and model theory; with student teams I work on how to evaluate machine reasoning, on quantum annealing, and on tools for teaching computation.

Areas

  • Computable structure theoryHow much computational power does it take to find an isomorphism between two copies of a countable structure, and how complex must a sentence be to describe the structure completely? My thesis studied degrees of categoricity and the isomorphism problem; current work with M. Mirabi studies the Scott complexity of trees of finite rank.
  • Vaught’s conjectureA countable complete theory has either countably many or continuum many countable models, up to isomorphism, or so the conjecture says. I first worked on it in my MSc thesis through cylindric algebra; a 2026 preprint studies the structure of minimal counterexamples. It does not settle the conjecture.
  • Evaluating machine reasoningBenchmarks whose answer keys come from running a verified toolchain, not from someone’s judgement: for example, asking language models to act as interpreters for programs in a student-built language, where every correct answer is produced by execution. The pilot is complete; scaling it up is next.
  • Quantum annealingFormulations of graph isomorphism as quadratic optimization problems, which I derived and a student team tested on D-Wave’s Advantage2, and an honest look at how much of the work classical pruning does before the annealer starts.
  • Computing educationTools for teaching Turing machines, and how students use generative AI in computing courses.
